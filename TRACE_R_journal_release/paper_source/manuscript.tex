\author{Amir~Ghorbani%
\thanks{Amir Ghorbani is with the School of Engineering, RMIT University, Melbourne, VIC, Australia (e-mail: amir.ghorbani@rmit.edu.au).}%
\thanks{Code, configurations, and frozen evaluation protocols are available at \url{https://github.com/AmirNetwork/rmrnet-restoration/tree/trace-r-journal/TRACE_R_journal_release}.}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Road-inspection vehicles record camera settings, inertial motion, and vehicle state, yet restoration networks usually infer image corruption from pixels alone. This paper presents TRACE-R, a telemetry-conditioned restoration front end for road-defect detection. An exposure-centred capture record is converted into physically interpretable corruption evidence and fused with an image estimate according to sensor availability and reliability. The resulting state selects an internal restoration route and conditions lightweight spatial adapters throughout a shared backbone. The model returns one image and requires no inference-time degradation label. Under matched training and validation-only selection, TRACE-R is the strongest deployable method on two controlled road-damage benchmarks and achieves the highest primary AP50 on a native-resolution field sequence with synchronized camera and inertial records. Missing and deliberately inconsistent sensor-record interventions reduce accuracy, confirming that the gain depends on measured capture information rather than additional network capacity alone.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Image restoration, inertial sensing, multimodal perception, pavement inspection, road-defect detection.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{V}{ehicle-mounted} cameras can inspect long road corridors without placing personnel in traffic. Their usefulness, however, depends on the evidence delivered to the detector. Motion during exposure spreads narrow cracks over neighbouring pixels, defocus weakens pothole rims, and low illumination couples longer exposure with sensor noise. These effects can coexist, and an enhancement that looks pleasing can still remove the texture needed for recognition.

Road-damage research has improved detector scale, domain adaptation, and shape representation~\cite{arya2022rdd2022,lin2023dardd,li2024yoloxrdd,zhang2024shapeadaptation}. These advances generally assume that a usable image reaches the detector. Restoration can supply a front end, but blind restoration must infer both the corruption and the missing content from the same pixels. A soft pavement patch can be caused by camera rotation, focus error, or weak illumination; applying the wrong correction can amplify shadows or erase a crack.

Road-inspection vehicles already measure part of the missing cause. Camera exposure, gain, and focus state describe image formation; inertial samples describe motion during exposure; vehicle and timing records indicate whether those measurements are trustworthy. Such measurements are available before any defect label is produced. The opportunity is therefore not merely to append metadata to an image network, but to convert asynchronous and sometimes incomplete records into corruption evidence that can be checked against the image.

An earlier study, RMR-P~\cite{ghorbani2026rmrp}, provided initial evidence that capture metadata can help pavement-image restoration. Its fixed summary code, however, could not represent a sensor trajectory during exposure, distinguish a missing measurement from a genuine zero, or decide when image evidence should override an unreliable record. These limitations motivate the present question: how can a restoration network use the measurements that accompany each image without becoming dependent on a complete or perfectly calibrated sensor packet? \tracer answers this question by aligning the measurements to the exposure, converting them into cause-specific evidence, reconciling that evidence with an image estimate, and adapting a shared restorer at multiple scales. The experiments instantiate the restorer with DeMoE, but the sensing and adaptation formulation is not tied to that backbone.

The principal contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a capture-state formulation that converts synchronized camera, inertial, and vehicle measurements into corruption evidence while accounting for missing and unreliable records;
  \item a restoration model that reconciles measured evidence with visual evidence and conditions a shared multi-scale backbone, falling back to image evidence for unsupported corruption causes; and
  \item a matched evaluation on two controlled road-damage benchmarks and a native-resolution field dataset, together with direct sensor-record interventions that test whether the measured information is actually used.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Automated road inspection has progressed from mobile-camera detection~\cite{maeda2018road} to geographically diverse benchmarks such as RDD2022~\cite{arya2022rdd2022}. Current work addresses cross-country domain shift~\cite{lin2023dardd}, small and rare defects~\cite{su2022fsrdd}, front-view imagery~\cite{li2024yoloxrdd}, irregular defect geometry~\cite{zhang2024shapeadaptation}, shadows~\cite{kong2025shadowrobust}, and crack topology~\cite{jing2025topologycrack}. Low-light road-scene enhancement has also been coupled to semantic perception~\cite{wang2022sfnetn}. These methods show that acquisition quality affects automated inspection, but most treat the image as the complete observation.

Single-image restoration first developed strong corruption-specific models and then shared architectures for unknown or mixed degradation. Restormer uses efficient attention for high-resolution restoration~\cite{zamir2022restormer}, NAFNet shows that a carefully designed activation-free network is a competitive general baseline~\cite{chen2022nafnet}, and AirNet learns an image-derived degradation representation for all-in-one restoration~\cite{li2022airnet}. PromptIR conditions a shared network with learned degradation prompts~\cite{potlapalli2023promptir}; InstructIR accepts a natural-language restoration instruction~\cite{conde2024instructir}; DFPIR uses a text description to guide feature perturbation~\cite{tian2025dfpir}; and DeMoE routes image features through degradation-specialized experts~\cite{feijoo2025demoe}. DeMoE is used as the present backbone because its expert structure provides strong image-only routes for several blur and illumination conditions. It is also the most direct control for asking whether measured capture state improves routing beyond image evidence alone.

Restoration quality and recognition utility need not have the same ordering. TogetherNet jointly studies enhancement and detection~\cite{wang2022togethernet}, recognition-driven restoration aligns recovered content with semantic features~\cite{yang2023vrdir}, and UniRestore uses a diffusion prior to balance perceptual and task-oriented restoration~\cite{chen2025unirestore}. These approaches motivate detector-aware learning, but detector gradients alone can favour brittle high-frequency changes. The present objective therefore anchors restoration to the paired image and edge structure while using frozen detector features and supervised detector evidence as secondary terms.

External sensing supplies information that pixels cannot always identify. Gyroscope-assisted deblurring constrains the camera trajectory during exposure~\cite{joshi2010imu,hu2016smartphoneimu,yang2025gyro}, and event-guided methods similarly exploit measurements with higher temporal resolution than conventional frames~\cite{kim2022eventexposure,kim2024cmta}. Existing sensor-guided deblurring is primarily concerned with one corruption and assumes a usable auxiliary stream. The unresolved road-inspection problem is broader: camera, inertial, and vehicle records can be asynchronous, partially absent, and informative for different causes. \tracer fills this gap by assigning each modality explicit availability and reliability, checking sensor evidence against an image estimate, and applying the resulting state inside one multi-degradation restoration model.

\section{TRACE-R Method}
Let $I_d\in[0,1]^{3\times H\times W}$ be the observed road image and $I_c$ its clean target when paired training data are available. Measurements collected around the same exposure are grouped as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}=\{m_C,M_I,m_V\},
\label{eq:packetgroups}
\end{equation}
where $m_C$ contains camera settings, $M_I$ is a short inertial trajectory, and $m_V$ contains vehicle and synchronization context. The deployed mapping is
\begin{equation}
I_r=f_\theta(I_d,\mathcal{M}),
\label{eq:system}
\end{equation}
where $I_r$ is the restored image supplied to the road-defect detector. The same mapping is used when one or more measurement groups are absent. Table~\ref{tab:notation} defines the principal symbols, and Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} shows their relation.

\paragraph{Exposure alignment and partial records.}
Camera settings describe one exposure, whereas an IMU and vehicle bus produce continuous streams. TRACE-R first aligns those streams to the exposure interval and represents each trajectory at $T$ normalized temporal positions. This makes records with different sampling rates comparable while retaining motion direction and variation during image formation. For example, angular motion is relevant only through the motion accumulated while the shutter is open; the same angular rate implies less image displacement at a shorter exposure. The method is defined for general $T$. The finite record used in the experiments is one serialization of this time window, documented in Appendix~\ref{app:packet}, rather than an intrinsic 82-dimensional model requirement.

Real acquisition logs are not uniformly complete. For each modality group $g\in\{C,I,V\}$, an availability indicator $a_g$ states whether its values exist and a continuous reliability $r_g$ summarizes synchronization, measurement noise, autofocus confidence, or source quality. These variables are status information, not restoration commands. Missing values are zero-filled only after normalization and are always accompanied by $a_g=0$. Thus zero angular rate is distinguishable from a missing IMU, and reliable camera settings can still contribute when the vehicle record is absent.

\paragraph{Image--sensor corruption state.}
An image encoder estimates a bounded cause vector $z_I=g_I(I_d)$. Its coordinates describe motion, vibration, defocus, noise, weak illumination, compression, and aggregate severity. A physical map $h_{\rm phy}$ converts supported measurements to the same coordinates. For example, camera rotation during the exposure is summarized by
\begin{equation}
\Delta\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\boldsymbol{\omega}(t)\,dt,
\label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}
while exposure, gain, and focus fields initialize illumination, noise, and defocus evidence. Acceleration variation contributes to vibration. A learned residual accommodates calibration and sensor-to-camera mismatch without discarding this physical initialization:

\begin{equation}
z_m=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(h_{\rm phy}(\mathcal{M})+\epsilon_m R\odot\tanh \Delta h_\psi(\mathcal{M}),0,1\right),
\label{eq:sensorcode}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is assembled from the availability and reliability of the measurements supporting each cause. The residual branch is zero-initialized and bounded by $\epsilon_m$.

The model next estimates cause-wise confidence $q$ from $R$ and image--sensor agreement. Its posterior is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
z_0 &= q\odot z_m+(1-q)\odot z_I,\\
z &= \operatorname{clip}\!\left(z_0+\epsilon_p\tanh P_\psi[z_I,z_m,|z_m-z_I|],0,1\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
If a cause has no supported measurement, the corresponding $q$ is forced to zero and that coordinate follows $z_I$. The adapter state is
\begin{equation}
s=[z_I,z_m,|z_m-z_I|,q].
\label{eq:jointstate}
\end{equation}
The disagreement term allows the model to reject a temporally misaligned or physically inconsistent record instead of treating metadata as an unconditional switch.

\paragraph{Backbone conditioning.}
Let $B$ be a multi-scale restoration backbone with feature $x_l$ at depth $l$. TRACE-R uses supported coordinates of $z$ to bias any internal degradation route exposed by $B$; when support is weak, the backbone's image-derived route is retained. To influence restoration continuously rather than only through a discrete route, low-rank adapters are inserted at selected encoder, bottleneck, and decoder depths:
\begin{equation}
x'_l=x_l+a_l(s,q)P_l\,\sigma\!\left(D_l(Q_l\mathcal{N}(x_l);s)\right),
\label{eq:adapter}
\end{equation}
where $Q_l$ and $P_l$ are low-rank $1\times1$ projections, $D_l$ is a depthwise spatial operator with local and dilated kernels, and $\mathcal{N}$ is group normalization. State-dependent affine parameters modulate the rank-reduced features. The compatibility gate is
\begin{equation}
a_l(s,q)=\eta_l\,\sigma(w_l^\top s+b_l)\left(\delta+(1-\delta)\max_j q_j\right),
\label{eq:adaptergate}
\end{equation}
where $\delta$ retains a bounded image-conditioned path when telemetry is absent. A learned spatial gate acts inside $D_l$. The output projection is initialized to zero, so adding TRACE-R initially preserves the backbone function. The present implementation instantiates $B$ with DeMoE and distributes adapters across its encoder, bottleneck, and decoder. Appendix~\ref{app:training} records the implementation-specific depths, rank, gains, and routing thresholds.

\paragraph{Learning objective.}
The fidelity term anchors the output to the paired target and its local structure:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm fid}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\sqrt{(I_r^i-I_c^i)^2+\varepsilon^2}
+\lambda_g\|\nabla I_r-\nabla I_c\|_1.
\label{eq:fidelity}
\end{equation}
Here $N$ is the number of image elements and $\varepsilon$ is the Charbonnier smoothing constant. This robust term limits the influence of a small number of imperfect crops; the gradient term protects narrow cracks and pothole rims.

A frozen dataset-specific detector supplies two secondary signals. Task-driven perceptual loss matches normalized intermediate features,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm TDP}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\alpha_k
\|\widehat{\Phi}_k(I_r)-\operatorname{sg}(\widehat{\Phi}_k(I_c))\|_2^2,
\label{eq:tdp}
\end{equation}
where hats denote channel-normalized features, $\alpha_k$ weights detector layer $k$, and $\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients through the clean branch. The supervised term is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm det}=\kappa_c\mathcal{L}_{\rm cls}
+\kappa_b\mathcal{L}_{\rm box}+\kappa_f\mathcal{L}_{\rm DFL},
\label{eq:detectorloss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\rm cls}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\rm box}$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\rm DFL}$ are the detector's classification, box-regression, and distribution-focal losses on restored training crops, and $\kappa_c,\kappa_b,\kappa_f$ are their fixed weights. Detector parameters remain frozen in both terms.

The image, sensor, and posterior codes are supervised by the known training corruption state $z^*$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm state}=\sum_{u\in\{z_I,z_m,z\}}\rho_u
\operatorname{SmoothL1}(u,z^*).
\label{eq:stateloss}
\end{equation}
Here $\rho_u$ balances the image, sensor, and joint estimates; unsupported sensor coordinates are masked rather than assigned a guessed target. The observable physical target $z^*_{\rm phy}$ contains only quantities derivable from the training capture record. With support mask $A$, its calibration loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm phy}={}&
\sum_{u\in\{z_I,z_m,z\}}\frac{\nu_u}{\max(1,\|A_u\|_1)}\\
&\times\sum_j A_{u,j}\operatorname{SmoothL1}
\!\left(u_{{\rm phy},j},z^*_{{\rm phy},j}\right),
\end{split}
\label{eq:physicalloss}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_u$ weights the three estimates and $A_u$ combines physical observability with cause-wise sensor support. The detector classification, localization, state, and physical terms are therefore all defined on training targets. The complete objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm fid}
+\lambda_t\mathcal{L}_{\rm TDP}
+\lambda_d\mathcal{L}_{\rm det}
+\lambda_s\mathcal{L}_{\rm state}
+\lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{\rm phy}.
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Training independently drops camera, IMU, and vehicle groups, perturbs available measurements, and occasionally pairs an image with an inconsistent record. These interventions teach partial-packet use and rejection of unsupported telemetry. At inference the model automatically emits $I_r$; there is no manual metadata mode or post-restoration choice. The frozen coefficients and optimization schedule are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:training}.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Controlled Road-Damage Benchmarks}
IVCNZ contains pothole imagery; PCM contains pothole, crack, and manhole annotations~\cite{giordani2025roadpcm}. Source and temporal groups are separated before rendering. The training partitions contain 731 IVCNZ and 1,402 PCM source images, and the validation partitions contain 146 and 353 source images. Four per-image corruption families are rendered: motion, defocus, low light, and compound motion--low light. Each sidecar stores only observable virtual camera/IMU measurements. Test identities remain inaccessible until the validation-selected checkpoint and all comparator policies are frozen.

The detector is YOLO11s~\cite{ultralytics2023yolo}, trained on the clean training split for each dataset and then frozen. Detection is evaluated on restored images with COCO-style mAP50 and mAP50--95. Clean-test detector accuracy is 0.424 mAP50 on IVCNZ and 0.334 on PCM, providing an upper reference for the corruption study. Paired fidelity is measured by PSNR and SSIM.

\subsection{Comparators and Matched Adaptation}
The comparison includes the authors' released GoPro width-32 NAFNet~\cite{chen2022nafnet}, InstructIR~\cite{conde2024instructir}, DFPIR~\cite{tian2025dfpir}, and DeMoE~\cite{feijoo2025demoe}. These models respectively represent a strong image-only architecture, instruction-guided restoration, degradation-prompted restoration, and image-routed expert restoration. The official NAFNet architecture and released tensors are loaded strictly, then one model is adapted to the common corruption mixture; using a separately selected NAFNet for each condition would require an inference-time condition label. TRACE-R and DeMoE share the same restoration backbone, making DeMoE-auto the closest capacity control.

Every trainable method receives the same controlled training sources, balanced defect-crop stream, continuation epochs, optimizer-update budget, and frozen dataset-specific detectors. Generated training conditions can supervise a baseline's prompt or expert, but inference information differs as shown in Table~\ref{tab:inference_inputs}. DFPIR and InstructIR are condition-informed controls in the controlled experiment, whereas DeMoE-auto and NAFNet infer from the image alone. DeMoE-oracle receives the rendered condition directly as an expert-selection label and is kept separate as a non-deployable upper bound.

\subsection{CRID Native-Resolution Field Study}
The Collingwood Road Inspection Dataset (CRID) contains 4,134 native 4752$\times$3168 Sony ILX-LR1 frames acquired from a survey vehicle with SBG navigation and inertial measurements. Forty-six frames have manually checked road-defect boxes. The strict synchronized subset comprises 25 annotated frames with direct camera--SBG alignment: an earlier 12-frame segment fixes the restoration strength using the predeclared relaxed-overlap calibration score, and a disjoint later 13-frame segment containing 49 defects is evaluated once. No synthetic degradation is added, and all images and boxes remain in native coordinates.

The frozen field detector uses the YOLO26 family~\cite{jocher2026yolo26} initialized from a public road-damage checkpoint~\cite{tamako2026yolo26rddfrdc}. Every restorer uses the same detector, resize rule, confidence floor, and class-aware suppression. Pooled AP50 over the four mapped road-defect classes is the headline field metric. AP10 is retained because it was the validation calibration score and because long crack annotations and fragmented predictions can overlap weakly even when they refer to the same defect. Unlike the controlled mAP50, pooled field AP50 is not an average of per-class AP values; the two experiments therefore use the same 0.50 localization threshold but different aggregation because the field class counts are sparse.

\section{Discussion}
Three observations are central. First, capture telemetry is useful only when it is tied to image evidence. The unavailable-packet result remains competitive because $z_I$ and the backbone's image route stay active; the wrong-packet reduction shows that the model has not learned a generic gain from an extra vector. Second, the SSIM--mAP and PSNR--mAP rankings differ. Minimizing pixel error can favour conservative smoothing, whereas detector-aware adaptation preserves defect structure. Third, the sensor state must influence both route selection and feature processing. Cause-aware routing chooses a suitable restoration regime, while the distributed adapters adjust its strength and spatial response continuously.

The physical code should be interpreted as a calibrated prior, not an exact blur kernel. Camera rotation during exposure is directly relevant to image motion, but acceleration, speed, and yaw provide context rather than depth-complete optical flow. Equation~\eqref{eq:posterior} therefore exposes reliability and disagreement to the learned posterior. This also explains why partial packets are useful: reliable exposure and focus fields can contribute when IMU data are absent, while measured angular rate can contribute when lens metadata are unavailable.

The principal limitations are data scale and sensor alignment. Controlled corruption provides paired clean targets and exact observable virtual records, but cannot cover all pavement, weather, lens, and rolling-shutter interactions. CRID provides real camera and inertial measurements, yet the annotated temporal block is small. The method also assumes that timestamps and calibration quality can be summarized by the supplied reliability fields. Large offsets, unmodelled camera--IMU extrinsics, or a detector outside the training domain can still reduce performance. In deployment, native images and capture logs should be retained for audit, restored outputs should be marked as derived data, and low-confidence detections should remain reviewable by an operator.

\section{Conclusion}
\tracer connects observable camera, inertial, and vehicle measurements to a single restoration network for road-defect detection. A physical sensor map, an image estimate, and explicit measurement reliability form a joint corruption state that conditions the restoration backbone. Under matched training and validation-only selection, the method gives the strongest deployable detection result on IVCNZ and PCM and the highest pooled AP50 in the native-resolution CRID field block. Sensor-record interventions confirm that aligned measurements, rather than model capacity alone, account for part of the gain. The remaining oracle gap and limited field scale define the next validation priorities.

\section{Capture Packet and State Construction}
\label{app:packet}
The method in Section III accepts an inertial trajectory of general length $T$. The released implementation uses $T=11$ because the field SBG unit records at 200~Hz and the 50~ms capture-centred support contains ten 5~ms intervals and their two endpoints. Linear interpolation evaluates angular rate and acceleration at these common temporal positions. This choice is a sampling convention, not a learned corruption label or an intrinsic dimensionality of TRACE-R.

The resulting implementation record has 82 scalar values: $11\times3$ angular-rate values, $11\times3$ acceleration values, and 16 context values. The context fields are log exposure, log ISO, analog gain, focal length, aperture, focus-error proxy, autofocus confidence, rolling-shutter readout, vehicle speed, vehicle yaw rate, timestamp offset, IMU noise, three modality-reliability values, and an overall availability indicator. IMU values are normalized to calibrated full scale and remain signed. Camera settings and quality values lie in $[0,1]$; timestamp offset and vehicle yaw rate remain signed. The motion map integrates angular rate over exposure and combines inertial variation with vehicle context; focus, noise, and low-light coordinates use the supported lens, gain, ISO, and exposure fields. Neither the 82-dimensional record nor its field count is presented to the restoration backbone as a degradation class: it is encoded into the cause state of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sensorcode}.

Availability is indexed by the three-bit pattern $4a_C+2a_I+a_V$. Each pattern has a small zero-initialized calibration residual, but reliability remains continuous. This separation prevents a camera+IMU packet from being treated as equivalent to camera+vehicle data. Packet noise never alters availability fields, and dropped modalities remain zero after perturbation.

For controlled images, virtual gyroscope trajectories are derived from the sampled camera-motion path during exposure, and the sidecar records the sampled camera settings and uncertainty. The hidden renderer kernel, clean target, benchmark scenario, and detector label are not inference fields. For CRID, packet construction matches image timestamps to the 200-Hz SBG stream, samples an exposure-centred window, and records synchronization uncertainty. EXIF and navigation fields are normalized with training-set statistics saved in the release.

\section{Optimization and Selection Details}
\label{app:training}
The accepted run uses 32 continuation epochs, 512 balanced crops per epoch, $128\times128$ patches, effective batch size four, AdamW with initial learning rate $10^{-5}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, and 4096 optimizer updates. The low-rank sensor modules use a fivefold learning-rate multiplier. Adapter rank is 24 and maximum adapter gain is 0.35. The route thresholds for motion, defocus, and low light are 0.18, 0.20, and 0.385, selected before the test run.

In Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective}, $(\lambda_g,\lambda_t,\lambda_d,\lambda_s,\lambda_p)=(0.15,0.20,0.10,0.10,0.05)$ and $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$. The image, sensor, and joint state weights are $\rho=(0.5,0.5,1.0)$ and $\nu=(0.25,0.25,0.50)$. Detector features are taken from backbone layers \texttt{model.2} and \texttt{model.4}. The detector branch is frozen, and clean features are evaluated without gradient storage. Detector-aware terms warm up for one epoch. Defect-centred crops are sampled with probability 0.6; cross-quality patch mixing is applied to the supervised detector branch with probability 0.25.

Metadata robustness reaches its full setting after a four-epoch curriculum and four-epoch ramp. At full strength, modality dropout is 0.10, Gaussian measurement noise is 0.005 in normalized units, and packet mismatch probability is 0.05. Scenario-label teacher forcing is zero. Validation mAP50 alone selects the checkpoint; the test split does not select checkpoints, route thresholds, residual strength, or display examples.

All five trainable methods complete the same 32-epoch, 4096-update continuation. The release records their complete SHA-256 identities. The selected TRACE-R checkpoint is epoch 8 because it has the highest joint validation mAP50; its hash begins \texttt{a79e2a775e57}. The full run is retained to show that selection did not stop at the first favourable epoch.

The NAFNet comparison uses the authors' released GoPro width-32 configuration: encoder depths $(1,1,1,28)$, one middle block, decoder depths $(1,1,1,1)$, and 17.1 million parameters. The released tensors load strictly before matched adaptation. Because this deep activation-free stage is numerically unstable under mixed precision with the detector-aware objective, NAFNet is adapted in FP32; its data, crops, update count, loss weights, and validation criterion remain matched.

\section{CRID Protocol and Qualitative Selection}
\label{app:crid}
CRID restoration preserves the 4752$\times$3168 coordinate system. The selected \tracer output is $I_d+0.5(I_r-I_d)$, with the coefficient fixed by AP10 on the earlier 12-frame block before the later images were opened. The later 13-frame block contains 49 manually checked defects. AP uses a confidence floor of 0.001 and class-aware NMS at IoU 0.70. Every method uses the same frozen detector and exactly one image. No crop mask, detector-box merge, or ground-truth-dependent routing is applied.

The controlled qualitative frames are selected by a frozen rule over the sealed outputs: maximize \tracer's true-positive advantage over the strongest comparator, then its advantage over the comparator mean, then minimize \tracer false positives. Display confidence is 0.25 and matching IoU is 0.50. The CRID panel uses the same 0.50 matching criterion as the primary field result. The selection manifest stores the image identifiers, thresholds, detector hashes, and per-frame counts. Aggregate AP, not the selected image, is the evidentiary result.

\section{Reproducibility and Governance}
The release includes source code, normalized packet definitions, dataset builders, training arguments, selected checkpoint hashes, frozen split hashes, test ledgers, table/figure builders, and qualitative-selection manifests. Raw CRID imagery and precise location records require controlled release because street-level images may contain people, vehicle plates, and geolocation. A deployment should retain the native image, packet quality fields, model hash, restored derivative, detector output, and timestamp in one audit record. Failure review should distinguish restoration error, packet synchronization error, detector domain shift, and annotation ambiguity.
