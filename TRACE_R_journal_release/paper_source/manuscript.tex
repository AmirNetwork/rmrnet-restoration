\begin{abstract}
Road-inspection vehicles record camera settings, inertial motion, and vehicle state, while image-restoration networks usually estimate corruption from pixels alone. This paper presents TRACE-R, a telemetry-conditioned restoration front end for road-defect detection. TRACE-R aligns the measurements with each camera exposure, maps them to interpretable corruption evidence, and reconciles this evidence with an image-derived estimate according to sensor availability and reliability. The resulting state controls restoration routes and lightweight adapters within a shared backbone. Task-aware training preserves the image structure and detector evidence needed for pavement inspection. The deployed model returns one restored image and requires no degradation label. Under matched training and validation-based selection, TRACE-R provides the strongest deployable detection result on two controlled road-damage benchmarks and the highest primary AP50 on a full-resolution field sequence with synchronized camera and inertial records. Capture-record interventions confirm that the improvement is associated with the measured exposure context.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Image restoration, inertial sensing, multimodal perception, pavement inspection, road-defect detection.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{V}{ehicle-mounted} cameras allow road agencies to inspect long corridors while reducing the need for personnel to work in traffic. The detector can only use evidence that survives image formation. Rotation during exposure spreads a narrow crack across neighbouring pixels, defocus weakens a pothole rim, and low illumination increases both exposure time and sensor noise. These effects often occur together and can make two physically different capture failures look similar in the image.

Current road-damage detectors address scale, class imbalance, domain shift, and irregular defect shape~\cite{arya2022rdd2022,lin2023dardd,li2024yoloxrdd,zhang2024shapeadaptation}. Their input is generally treated as a complete visual observation. A restoration front end can recover useful structure before detection, although a blind restorer must infer the corruption and the missing detail from the same pixels. When a soft pavement region could arise from motion, focus, or illumination, an incorrect correction may strengthen shadows or remove fine crack texture.

An instrumented inspection vehicle observes additional information during capture. Exposure, gain, aperture, and focus describe the camera state. An inertial measurement unit records motion across the exposure interval. Vehicle speed, timing error, and sensor quality indicate how confidently these measurements can be aligned with the image. These records provide evidence about the cause of corruption before the scene reaches a detector. Their value depends on converting asynchronous and partially available measurements into a form that can be compared with visual evidence.

The objective also differs from conventional photographic enhancement. Pavement restoration must preserve the scene and retain the structures used for localization and classification. A purely pixel-based objective can smooth a narrow crack. Strong detector supervision can produce unstable texture when it is applied without an image constraint. Effective inspection restoration therefore requires a coordinated objective in which image fidelity establishes the scene and task guidance protects detector-relevant evidence.

RMR-P~\cite{ghorbani2026rmrp} established the initial feasibility of metadata-aware pavement restoration using a fixed capture summary. TRACE-R develops this idea into an exposure-aligned and reliability-aware framework. It estimates corruption from the image and from measured capture data, reconciles the two estimates cause by cause, and conditions a shared restorer throughout its feature hierarchy. A frozen detector guides training through intermediate features and supervised localization loss. Inference remains automatic and returns one restored image for the existing inspection pipeline.

The principal contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item an exposure-aligned capture representation that translates camera, inertial, and vehicle measurements into cause-specific evidence with explicit availability and reliability;
  \item a reliability-aware image--sensor state that controls restoration routes and identity-initialized adapters across a general multi-scale backbone;
  \item a task-aware learning strategy that combines image fidelity, detector-feature preservation, localization supervision, and corruption-state calibration; and
  \item matched controlled evaluations and a full-resolution field study using the Collingwood Road Inspection Dataset (CRID), a new collection of synchronized high-resolution road imagery and vehicle telemetry.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Automated road inspection has advanced from mobile-camera detection~\cite{maeda2018road} to geographically diverse benchmarks such as RDD2022~\cite{arya2022rdd2022}. Recent methods improve cross-country transfer~\cite{lin2023dardd}, rare-defect recognition~\cite{su2022fsrdd}, front-view acquisition~\cite{li2024yoloxrdd}, irregular-shape modelling~\cite{zhang2024shapeadaptation}, shadow robustness~\cite{kong2025shadowrobust}, and crack connectivity~\cite{jing2025topologycrack}. This work has strengthened the detector and its representation of pavement damage. Low-light road-scene studies further show that acquisition quality affects semantic perception~\cite{wang2022sfnetn}. Together, these results motivate attention to the image supplied to the detector.

Modern restoration networks increasingly address several degradations with one model. Restormer uses efficient attention for high-resolution recovery~\cite{zamir2022restormer}, NAFNet provides a strong activation-free architecture~\cite{chen2022nafnet}, and AirNet learns a degradation representation from the observed image~\cite{li2022airnet}. PromptIR introduces learned restoration prompts~\cite{potlapalli2023promptir}; InstructIR follows a natural-language instruction~\cite{conde2024instructir}; DFPIR uses degradation-prompted feature perturbation~\cite{tian2025dfpir}; and DeMoE routes features through degradation-specialized experts~\cite{feijoo2025demoe}. These designs provide suitable multi-degradation backbones. Their restoration state is inferred from the image or supplied as a task description, leaving measured exposure conditions outside the restoration decision.

Task-aware restoration connects image recovery to recognition. TogetherNet jointly learns enhancement and detection~\cite{wang2022togethernet}, recognition-driven restoration preserves semantic features~\cite{yang2023vrdir}, SR4IR develops detector-feature supervision and cross-quality training~\cite{kim2024tdp}, and UniRestore balances perceptual and task objectives with a diffusion prior~\cite{chen2025unirestore}. This literature demonstrates that full-reference fidelity and downstream accuracy can rank restorers differently. TRACE-R follows this insight by using detector evidence during training while retaining explicit image and edge constraints.

Auxiliary sensors can resolve ambiguities that remain in a single visual stream. Inertial-assisted deblurring relates measured camera motion to the exposure~\cite{joshi2010imu,hu2016smartphoneimu,yang2025gyro}, and event-guided restoration uses a high-rate visual stream~\cite{kim2022eventexposure,kim2024cmta}. More broadly, multimodal ITS systems use complementary measurements to sustain perception when one modality becomes unreliable. Bandara \emph{et al.} combine audio and visual updates to maintain multi-object tracks during visual occlusion~\cite{bandara2026occlusion}. These studies establish the value of measurement reliability and modality-specific evidence.

Road-inspection restoration presents a distinct operational need. The system must interpret camera, inertial, and vehicle records that arrive at different rates; use the available part of a record; separate motion evidence from focus and illumination evidence; and improve the image consumed by an existing detector. Motion-specific sensor deblurring addresses one cause, while blind and prompted restorers provide no direct link to the measured exposure. TRACE-R addresses this missing capability through a reliability-aware capture state and a task-guided restoration interface.

\section{TRACE-R Method}
A road image provides strong evidence about visible texture and only indirect evidence about the capture process. TRACE-R complements that visual evidence with measurements recorded during the exposure. Its computation follows four stages: align the capture record, estimate the corruption from the image and measurements, reconcile the two estimates, and condition a multi-scale restoration backbone. Task-aware learning then preserves the structures needed by the road-defect detector. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes this sequence.

Let $I_d\in[0,1]^{3\times H\times W}$ denote the observed image and $I_c$ a clean reference when paired data are available. TRACE-R maps the image and its capture record $\mathcal{M}$ to one restored image,
\begin{equation}
I_r=f_\theta(I_d,\mathcal{M}).
\label{eq:system}
\end{equation}
The same mapping accepts complete and partial records. Availability and reliability are represented inside $\mathcal{M}$, so inference remains automatic. Table~\ref{tab:notation} defines the principal symbols.

\subsection{Exposure-Aligned Capture Record}
Camera settings describe one exposure, while an inertial measurement unit and vehicle bus produce time series. TRACE-R groups the available measurements as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}=\{m_C,M_I,m_V,a,r\},
\label{eq:packetgroups}
\end{equation}
where $m_C$ contains camera settings, $M_I$ contains inertial samples around the exposure, and $m_V$ contains vehicle motion and timing information. The vectors $a$ and $r$ describe modality availability and reliability. For modality $g\in\{C,I,V\}$, $a_g\in\{0,1\}$ records whether a measurement is present and $r_g\in[0,1]$ summarizes synchronization and measurement quality. This representation distinguishes a measured zero angular rate from an unavailable inertial record.

The inertial and vehicle trajectories are interpolated at normalized positions across the exposure support. Sampling relative to the shutter interval gives a common representation for different sensor rates and retains temporal direction. Exposure duration remains explicit because the same angular rate produces a different image displacement under a different shutter time.

\subsection{Image and Measurement Evidence}
TRACE-R represents corruption with eight normalized coordinates: horizontal motion, vertical motion, vibration, defocus, noise, low illumination, compression, and overall severity. An image encoder estimates this state from the observed pixels,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
z_I=g_I(I_d)=[&z_x^{\rm mot},z_y^{\rm mot},z^{\rm vib},z^{\rm def},\\
&z^{\rm noise},z^{\rm dark},z^{\rm comp},z^{\rm sev}],
\end{split}
\label{eq:imagecode}
\end{equation}
where $g_I$ is a compact convolutional encoder and the final coordinate summarizes the strongest estimated cause. The measurements provide a second estimate in the same coordinate system. The deterministic map $h_{\rm phy}$ converts supported physical quantities into normalized cause strengths. For motion, it first integrates the measured angular rate over the exposure,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=\int_{t_0}^{t_1}\boldsymbol{\omega}(t)\,dt,
\label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}
and then maps its magnitude and direction to horizontal, vertical, and vibrational evidence. Focus status supplies defocus evidence; exposure, ISO, and gain supply illumination and noise evidence. The compression coordinate is inferred from the image. The complete deterministic estimate is written as $z_{\rm phy}=h_{\rm phy}(\mathcal{M})\in[0,1]^8$.

Camera--IMU alignment and sensor scaling introduce calibration error. A zero-initialized residual network $\Delta h_\psi$ learns a bounded correction around the physical estimate,
\begin{equation}
z_m=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(z_{\rm phy}+r_{\mathcal M}\epsilon_m\tanh \Delta h_\psi(\mathcal{M},a),0,1\right),
\label{eq:sensorcode}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\mathcal M}$ summarizes record reliability and $\epsilon_m$ sets the maximum calibration range. Initialization gives $z_m=z_{\rm phy}$, and learning subsequently adjusts the conversion within the stated bound.

\subsection{Evidence Reconciliation and Backbone Conditioning}
The vector $q\in[0,1]^8$ gives the measurement support for each corruption coordinate. It combines availability, synchronization error, sensor noise, and camera quality. A reliable zero angular rate therefore provides strong evidence for low motion, while an unavailable inertial record contributes zero support.

Measurement-supported coordinates are drawn toward $z_m$ and the remaining coordinates retain $z_I$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\bar z &= q\odot z_m+(1-q)\odot z_I,\\
\Delta z_\psi &=\epsilon_p\tanh P_\psi([z_I,z_m,|z_m-z_I|]),\\
z &= \operatorname{clip}(\bar z+\Delta z_\psi,0,1).
\end{split}
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
The network $P_\psi:\mathbb{R}^{24}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{8}$ is a small multilayer perceptron. Its output $\Delta z_\psi$ is an eight-coordinate calibration of the reliability-weighted mixture. The final layer is initialized to zero and the scale $\epsilon_p$ bounds each correction. This construction gives an image-derived value to unsupported coordinates and a calibrated measurement value to well-supported coordinates.

Let $B=B_L\circ\cdots\circ B_1$ denote a differentiable multi-scale restoration backbone. Its feature recursion is
\begin{equation}
x_{l+1}=B_l(x'_l),\qquad x_0=I_d,
\label{eq:backbone}
\end{equation}
where $x_l$ is the feature entering stage $l$. TRACE-R inserts an identity-initialized residual adapter after selected encoder, bottleneck, and decoder stages:
\begin{equation}
x'_l=x_l+c_l(s,q)P_l\,\sigma\!\left(D_l(Q_l\mathcal{N}(x_l);s)\right),
\label{eq:adapter}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{N}$ normalizes the feature, $Q_l$ reduces its channel dimension, $D_l$ applies local and dilated depthwise filtering, and $P_l$ restores the original width. The evidence vector $s$ supplies affine scale and shift parameters to $D_l$. The scalar compatibility factor is
\begin{equation}
c_l(s,q)=\eta_l\,\sigma(w_l^\top s+b_l)\left(\delta+(1-\delta)\max_j q_j\right),
\label{eq:adaptergate}
\end{equation}
where $\eta_l$ bounds the adapter, the sigmoid measures compatibility, and $\delta$ retains a small image-conditioned response under sparse telemetry. The adapter evidence is
\[
s\equiv[z_I,z_m,|z_m-z_I|,q].
\]
It records the two estimates, their disagreement, and their support. Large disagreement combined with weak sensor support favours the image-derived response; reliable measured motion permits a stronger motion-conditioned correction. Zero initialization of $P_l$ makes every adapter an identity map at insertion.

For an expert backbone, $z$ also controls the expert route. In the controlled implementation, DeMoE supplies the shared multi-degradation backbone. TRACE-R derives the route from the calibrated state $z$ and places adapters at selected encoder, bottleneck, and decoder features. The field study uses the same interface with a DFPIR substrate selected on its validation block. Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:backbones} maps these two implementations to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:backbone}--\eqref{eq:adaptergate}.

\subsection{Task-Aware Learning}
Paired training uses the clean image, the capture state, and defect boxes $Y$. Image fidelity establishes the scene, detector losses protect inspection evidence, and state losses calibrate the capture interpretation. The fidelity term is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm fid}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i\sqrt{(I_r^i-I_c^i)^2+\varepsilon^2}
+\lambda_g\|\nabla I_r-\nabla I_c\|_1.
\label{eq:fidelity}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the number of image elements and $\varepsilon$ smooths the absolute error. The gradient term gives additional weight to narrow cracks and pothole rims.

A frozen detector supplies task evidence in two forms. Normalized intermediate features of the restored image are matched to the clean reference~\cite{kim2024tdp}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm TDP}=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\alpha_k
\|\widehat{\Phi}_k(I_r)-\stopgrad[\widehat{\Phi}_k(I_c)]\|_2^2,
\label{eq:tdp}
\end{equation}
where hats denote channel-normalized features, $\alpha_k$ weights detector layer $k$, and $\stopgrad[\cdot]$ treats the clean feature as a fixed target. The detector's supervised loss also compares predictions on the restored crop with $Y$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm det}=\kappa_c\mathcal{L}_{\rm cls}
+\kappa_b\mathcal{L}_{\rm box}+\kappa_f\mathcal{L}_{\rm DFL},
\label{eq:detectorloss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\rm cls}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\rm box}$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\rm DFL}$ are classification, box-regression, and distribution-focal losses. Detector parameters remain fixed while gradients update the restorer. Deployment uses the restored image as the sole output of this training path.

The corruption-state terms compare the image, measurement, and fused estimates with the training target $z^*$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm state}=\sum_{u\in\{z_I,z_m,z\}}\rho_u
\operatorname{SmoothL1}(u,z^*).
\label{eq:stateloss}
\end{equation}
The coefficients $\rho_u$ balance the three estimates, and the measurement mask selects supported coordinates. A second calibration uses physical quantities derived from the capture record. With target $z^*_{\rm phy}$ and support mask $A$, it is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm phy}={}&
\sum_{u\in\{z_I,z_m,z\}}\frac{\nu_u}{\max(1,\|A_u\|_1)}\\
&\times\sum_j A_{u,j}\operatorname{SmoothL1}
\!\left(u_{{\rm phy},j},z^*_{{\rm phy},j}\right),
\end{split}
\label{eq:physicalloss}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_u$ weights each estimate and $A_u$ selects causes observed by the available measurements. The complete paired-data objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm fid}
+\lambda_t\mathcal{L}_{\rm TDP}
+\lambda_d\mathcal{L}_{\rm det}
+\lambda_s\mathcal{L}_{\rm state}
+\lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{\rm phy}.
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Optimization follows a staged schedule. Fidelity begins first; detector terms enter after a short warm-up; and record dropout, perturbation, and mismatch are then increased through a curriculum. State calibration carries a smaller weight than the image and detector terms. Once the curriculum is complete, every term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} is optimized in the same update. Independent camera, IMU, and vehicle dropout teaches the model to use partial records.

\subsection{Calibration from Labelled Survey Frames}
Real surveys rarely provide a perfectly aligned clean exposure for each captured frame. They can provide a short calibration segment in which an operator reviews defect boxes. The field procedure therefore begins with a restorer trained on paired road data and refines it using the observed survey image $I_d$ and reviewed boxes $Y$.

The restored image is evaluated by a fixed detector, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:detectorloss} supplies the box-based gradient to the restorer. The observed image supplies the appearance reference through identity and edge constraints:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\rm field}={}&\lambda_d\mathcal{L}_{\rm det}(I_r,Y)
+\beta_0\rho(I_r-I_d)\\
&+\beta_1\|\nabla I_r-\nabla I_d\|_1
+\beta_2\|\nabla(I_r-I_d)\|_1,
\end{split}
\label{eq:fieldobjective}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the Charbonnier penalty. The first term strengthens evidence associated with the reviewed defects. The next two terms retain colour and visible boundaries from the survey frame. The final total-variation term regularizes the learned residual. This objective can refine any differentiable restorer because supervision is applied to its output image. A later temporal segment selects the checkpoint and the residual blend between the observed and restored images. The blend controls correction magnitude and is fixed for subsequent deployment.

After calibration, Eq.~\eqref{eq:system} remains the deployment model. It receives the image and available capture record, and it returns one restored image. The reviewed boxes and training detector are confined to calibration.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Controlled Road-Damage Benchmarks}
IVCNZ contains pothole imagery; PCM contains pothole, crack, and manhole annotations~\cite{giordani2025roadpcm}. Source and temporal groups are separated before rendering. The training partitions contain 731 IVCNZ and 1,402 PCM source images, and the validation partitions contain 146 and 353 source images. Four independently sampled corruption families are rendered per source: motion, defocus, low light, and compound motion--low light. Each rendering also produces a virtual capture record with noisy camera, inertial, and vehicle observations. The record emulates measurements available during capture; hidden rendering parameters and labels are excluded from model input. Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:controlled_generation} gives the renderer, record construction, and a complete input example. Test identities remain sealed until model selection is complete.

The detector is YOLO11s~\cite{ultralytics2023yolo}, trained on the clean training split for each dataset and then frozen. Detection is evaluated on restored images with COCO-style mAP50 and mAP50--95. Clean-test detector accuracy is 0.424 mAP50 on IVCNZ and 0.334 on PCM, providing an upper reference for the corruption study. Paired fidelity is measured by PSNR and SSIM.

\subsection{Comparators and Matched Training}
\label{sec:comparators}
The comparison includes NAFNet~\cite{chen2022nafnet}, InstructIR~\cite{conde2024instructir}, DFPIR~\cite{tian2025dfpir}, and DeMoE~\cite{feijoo2025demoe}. They represent image-only restoration, instruction-guided restoration, degradation-prompted feature perturbation, and image-routed experts. The released architectures and current source tensors are retained. NAFNet uses the published width-32 GoPro configuration; InstructIR retains its language-conditioning network; DFPIR retains its CLIP prompt encoder and perturbation blocks; and DeMoE retains its five-expert route. Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:backbones} records the exact configurations and weight coverage.

TRACE-R uses DeMoE as the controlled-study backbone because its expert routes give a strong response to motion, defocus, and illumination changes. DeMoE-auto is the matched image-only control with the same backbone. The difference between these models is the exposure-aligned capture state and its feature adapters.

Every trainable method receives the same controlled sources, balanced defect crops, 32-epoch continuation, 4,096 optimizer updates, and frozen dataset detector. InstructIR and DFPIR receive the rendered condition through their published instruction or prompt interface. NAFNet and DeMoE-auto infer the correction from the image. TRACE-R receives the observable virtual record. DeMoE-oracle receives the rendered corruption family as an expert label and is reported separately as a calibration upper bound. \subsection{CRID Full-Resolution Field Study}
The Collingwood Road Inspection Dataset (CRID) is a growing collection from a road-survey platform developed under Australia's National Road Safety Action Grants Program. It contains 4,134 native 4752$\times$3168 Sony ILX-LR1 frames synchronized with SBG navigation and inertial records. We reviewed 320 temporally ordered frames and marked 565 defect boxes. Fixed temporal blocks contain 180 frames for calibration, 60 for validation, and 80 for one-time testing. The test block contains 122 labelled defects; 40 of its frames were reviewed and contain no defect box, so they contribute directly to false-positive measurement. Images and annotations remain in native coordinates, and no synthetic corruption is added.

The CRID images are high-quality field observations acquired at low survey speed. The experiment therefore studies field calibration for defect evidence, using reviewed boxes in place of unavailable clean--degraded pairs. Each restorer begins from its own paired controlled-road checkpoint. During calibration, its output crop is evaluated by the same frozen CRID detector. Equation~\eqref{eq:fieldobjective} differentiates the box loss through that detector to the restorer, while the identity and edge terms keep the result close to the recorded frame. The reviewed boxes serve as calibration targets and never enter the restoration network. This output-level supervision applies uniformly to NAFNet, InstructIR, DFPIR, DeMoE, and TRACE-R.

All candidates use the same 180 calibration frames, crop stream, objective coefficients, and 7,200 optimizer updates. The following 60-frame block selects the checkpoint and residual blend. The final 80-frame block is evaluated once. Trainable scopes follow each architecture and are listed in Supplementary Section~\ref{sup:crid}. This protocol provides a practical route from a paired pretrained restorer to a field survey for which only a modest set of defect boxes is available.

Backbone selection for TRACE-R is also confined to validation. DeMoE is retained in the controlled experiment to isolate capture conditioning against a matched expert backbone. Among the field-calibrated candidates, DFPIR gives the highest mAP50 on the 60-frame CRID validation block and is therefore used as $B$ for the field instance. The direct DFPIR row and TRACE-R share this image substrate. TRACE-R additionally applies the exposure-aligned Sony--SBG record through its reliability policy. Feature adapters remain at identity for this small, already-sharp calibration set, and the automatic record-conditioned output policy is frozen before test access.

The field detector uses the YOLO26 family~\cite{jocher2026yolo26}, initialized from a public road-damage checkpoint~\cite{tamako2026yolo26rddfrdc}, calibrated on the 180-frame block, and selected on validation. Every test image uses the same frozen detector, resize rule, confidence floor, and class-aware suppression. The primary measure is confidence-integrated mAP50; mAP50--95 summarizes localization over stricter overlaps. Precision and recall are reported at the evaluator's maximum-F1 operating point. A DeMoE oracle route is unavailable in CRID because field images have no rendered corruption-family label.

\section{Discussion}
Three observations are central. First, capture telemetry is useful only when it is tied to image evidence. The unavailable-record result remains competitive because $z_I$ and the backbone's image route stay active; the inconsistent-record reduction shows that the model has not learned a generic gain from an extra vector. Second, the SSIM--mAP and PSNR--mAP rankings differ. Minimizing pixel error can favour conservative smoothing, whereas detector-aware adaptation preserves defect structure. Third, the sensor state must influence both route selection and feature processing. Cause-aware routing chooses a suitable restoration regime, while the distributed adapters adjust its strength and spatial response continuously.

The backbone choice follows the evidence available in each setting. The paired controlled study uses DeMoE to isolate capture-state conditioning against the same image-routed expert backbone. Field transfer treats $B$ as the replaceable interface defined in Section~III and selects it on a disjoint calibration sequence. DFPIR has the highest CRID validation mAP50 and is retained before test access. The field result therefore demonstrates transfer of the TRACE-R state and reliability interface to a conservative prompt-based substrate. DeMoE's higher test mAP50--95 identifies tighter localization as an important direction for field transfer.

The physical code is a calibrated capture prior. Camera rotation during exposure is directly related to image motion; acceleration, speed, and yaw provide wider context whose image-plane effect also depends on scene depth. Equation~\eqref{eq:posterior} exposes measurement support and disagreement to the learned correction. This formulation also explains the value of partial records: reliable exposure and focus fields can contribute when IMU data are absent, and measured angular rate can contribute when lens metadata are unavailable.

The principal limitations are sensor alignment and domain coverage. Controlled corruption provides paired clean targets and observable virtual records across a defined range of pavement, weather, lens, and rolling-shutter effects. CRID adds real camera and inertial measurements, with the current detection analysis covering one human-reviewed 320-frame route segment. Timing and calibration quality are summarized by the supplied reliability fields; large offsets, unmodelled camera--IMU extrinsics, and detector domain shift can reduce performance. Deployment should retain native images and capture logs for audit, mark restored outputs as derived data, and keep low-confidence detections available for operator review.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper introduced \tracer, a restoration front end that uses the measurements already recorded by an inspection vehicle. The method aligns camera, inertial, and vehicle records to an exposure, maps supported measurements and image evidence into a common corruption state, and conditions one multi-scale restorer according to their reliability and agreement. A staged task-aware objective preserves paired image structure while teaching the restored output to retain features and localization evidence used by a fixed road-defect detector.

Under matched data, optimization budgets, and validation-only selection, \tracer provides the strongest deployable detection result on the IVCNZ and PCM controlled tests. A separately calibrated field instance also achieves the highest primary mAP50 on the sealed CRID block using real Sony and SBG records, despite the absence of paired clean targets. Removing or contradicting the capture record reduces controlled validation accuracy, directly supporting the role of measured capture state. These results position telemetry-conditioned restoration as a practical complement to detector development: it improves the evidence delivered to an existing detector while retaining one auditable image output. The remaining oracle gap, cross-session sensor calibration, and wider CRID annotation are the most important directions for subsequent validation.

\section{Capture Record Serialization}
\label{sup:packet}
The capture record $\mathcal{M}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:packetgroups} is defined by measurement type, timestamp, availability, and reliability. This interface accepts sensor trajectories of different rates and durations. A deployment may therefore change the number of temporal samples while preserving the physical meaning of each field.

The evaluated implementation samples each inertial trajectory at eleven exposure-centred positions. The SBG unit records at 200~Hz, so a 50~ms support contains eleven positions at 5~ms spacing. Linear interpolation places angular rate and acceleration on these positions. The resulting serialization has 66 trajectory values and 16 context values. The context contains log exposure, log ISO, analog gain, focal length, aperture, focus-error proxy, autofocus confidence, rolling-shutter readout, vehicle speed, vehicle yaw rate, timestamp offset, IMU noise, three modality reliabilities, and overall availability. The resulting 82 values are an evaluated sensor-packet layout; the corruption state remains the eight-coordinate vector $z$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:posterior}.

Inertial quantities are normalized to calibrated full scale and remain signed. Camera and quality variables lie in $[0,1]$; timing offset and yaw rate retain their sign. Availability uses the three-bit pattern $4a_C+2a_I+a_V$, and reliability remains continuous. For CRID, each image timestamp is matched to the 200-Hz SBG stream, the exposure-centred window is sampled, and synchronization uncertainty is recorded. EXIF and navigation fields use normalization statistics fixed on the calibration data.

\section{Controlled Corruption and Virtual Records}
\label{sup:controlled_generation}
Clean IVCNZ and PCM source images are rendered independently under four families. Motion uses a normalized line-spread function with odd length sampled from 7--19 pixels and angle from $[-30^\circ,30^\circ]$. Defocus uses Gaussian blur with standard deviation 1.1--3.4 pixels. Low light applies gain 0.32--0.70, gamma 1.05--1.55, and additive Gaussian noise with standard deviation 2--8 in 8-bit intensity units. The compound condition first applies 5--15 pixel motion at $[-45^\circ,45^\circ]$ and then low-light gain 0.38--0.72, gamma 1.05--1.45, and noise standard deviation 2--9. A split-seeded generator keeps source identities and realizations distinct across training, validation, and test.

The virtual record emulates observations from a synchronized capture system. Exposure and ISO follow the illumination setting; focal length and aperture span 28--50~mm and $f/2.8$--$f/8$; focus error and autofocus confidence are noisy functions of rendered defocus. Motion displacement is converted to an exposure-normalized angular trajectory and perturbed by inertial noise and timestamp error. Split-disjoint KITTI OXTS drives supply realistic temporal covariance for acceleration, speed, and yaw context~\cite{geiger2012kitti}. The road image, annotation, and corruption realization still come from IVCNZ or PCM.

For example, one released IVCNZ validation item supplies a degraded RGB image together with 19.21~ms exposure, ISO 604, 40.81~mm focal length at $f/3.89$, eleven angular-rate and acceleration samples, 4.01~m/s vehicle speed, 0.158~rad/s yaw rate, $-0.087$~ms timestamp offset, and camera/IMU/vehicle reliabilities of 0.942/0.927/0.927. TRACE-R receives these measurements and their availability fields. The exact blur kernel, clean target, corruption-family name, and detector annotation remain outside the inference input.

\section{Optimization and Selection}
\label{sup:training}
The controlled run uses 32 continuation epochs, 512 balanced crops per epoch, $128\times128$ patches, effective batch size four, AdamW with initial learning rate $10^{-5}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, and 4,096 optimizer updates. Capture-state modules use a fivefold learning-rate multiplier because the backbone begins from released restoration weights and the added modules begin near identity. Adapter rank is 24 and maximum adapter gain is 0.35.

In Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective}, $(\lambda_g,\lambda_t,\lambda_d,\lambda_s,\lambda_p)=(0.15,0.20,0.10,0.10,0.05)$ and $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$. The image, measurement, and joint state weights are $\rho=(0.5,0.5,1.0)$ and $\nu=(0.25,0.25,0.50)$. Detector features come from the second and fourth stages. Detector-aware terms enter after one warm-up epoch. Defect-centred crops are sampled with probability 0.6, and cross-quality patch mixing is applied to the supervised detector branch with probability 0.25.

Capture-record robustness reaches full strength after four aligned-record epochs and a four-epoch ramp. The final probabilities are 0.10 for modality dropout and 0.05 for record mismatch; Gaussian measurement noise has standard deviation 0.005 in normalized units. Fidelity first establishes a stable image path, detector terms then preserve task evidence, and state terms calibrate supported and contradictory records. All terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} share each update after the ramp. Mean validation mAP50 selects the checkpoint. Every trainable comparator uses the same sources, update count, and validation criterion.
